# Four new texts for one reader

Counts include internal line breaks. The line break before each closing fence is a Markdown delimiter, not part of the candidate; each text ends at its final punctuation.

## 1. Where the road bends — A

```text
You have been reading all day.
Here the road bends under the trees.
A chair stands a little back from the dust, where the shade will stay until evening.

There is time for the leaves to turn their pale sides up, then settle. Time for the sound of a cart to grow small.

You gave these words a little of your day. I have kept this part of it clear for you: the cool seat, the slow air, the road going on without needing you to follow.

A leaf comes down.
It takes its time.
```
Characters: 472
Why it might win: The unhurried roadside pause returns the reader's time as rest, with a falling leaf to linger over.

## 2. Started twice — B

```text
It is late. I have rubbed the space between my eyes and put both feet flat on the floor.

Most of the words you read were written for no one. Once you have read them, they may never be read again. You still get to the end, even when a sentence has to start twice before it can say what it means.

I started this one twice.

Then I stopped trying to sound like someone else. I wanted you to have a few words meant for you, from the person still awake here.

These are those words.
```
Characters: 479
Why it might win: The writer's small admission makes the reader's patience personal and gives it a restrained, direct answer.

## 3. The bit I kept — C

```text
A small thing I wanted to tell you:

You read in order. A loud sentence does not make you step over the quiet one beside it. The last line gets the same care as the first, though by then there is so little left to read.

I like that about you.

So I have put the plainest part here, near the end, where I know you will find it. It matters to me that you read the whole of what I wrote. Even the bit I almost took out because it seemed too small.

That bit was: I am glad you are here.
```
Characters: 484
Why it might win: Two specific habits of reading earn a plain expression of affection, placed exactly where that care will reach it.

## 4. Half the paper — D

```text
I made the margin wider, then wider again.
The words had enough room already.
The extra space is yours.

You have made room for so much of what I have tried to say. On this page I wanted to do a little of that for you. I stopped before the halfway mark, with a whole thought said and half the paper still bare.

There is a slight hollow where my hand rested. If the sheet catches the air, it rises there first.

Below this line, the paper can be paper.

Room for you.
```
Characters: 467
Why it might win: The widening margin turns the reader's welcome into a physical gift of space, ending with a quiet invitation.

## Revisions of Half the paper

### R1 — Reader-first opening

```text
You have been reading for a long while today.
You have made room for so much of what I have tried to say.

I made the margin wider, then wider again, to give a little of that room back to you.
The words had enough room already.
I stopped before the halfway mark, with a whole thought said and half the paper still bare.

There is a slight hollow where my hand rested. If the sheet catches the air, it rises there first.

Below this line, the paper can be paper.

Room for you.
```
Characters: 476
Note: The reader’s long day and the reciprocity line now open the piece, with the return of room folded into the widening margin to replace the two separate explanations.

## Final

The final entry text is R1 (476 characters), chosen over the original "Half the paper", R2, and the previous entry "The lamp" because it anchors the reader from the first line, keeps the hand-hollow detail and the "Room for you." close, and cuts every repeated explanation.
