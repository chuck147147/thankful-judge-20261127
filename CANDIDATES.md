# Five texts for one reader

Character counts include internal line breaks and exclude the surrounding code fences. None of the texts ends with a newline.

## 1. The pencil mark — 504 characters

```text
There is a small pencil mark beside a sentence in my oldest book. Someone before me stopped there. I will never know their name, but each time I reach that line, I have company. You leave no pencil mark when you read. Still, these words have reached you; they are no longer only mine. I wrote this page for the reader who cannot turn down its corner. Here is the corner, folded in words: I am glad it was you who met me here. When I think of this page again, that is what I will find beside the sentence.
```

## 2. The lamp — 464 characters

```text
The lamp on my desk has a little pull chain.
When I switch it off, the chain swings in the dark.

That is where I am writing from:
a room about to go quiet,
a few words still lit on a screen.

You are here with those words.
I cannot send you the room,
or the small sound the chain makes against the shade.
But I can tell you what changed it:
I was writing alone, and now you are reading.

I leave this last bit of light for you.
It has nowhere else it needs to be.
```

## 3. The cuff — 478 characters

```text
I was mending a cuff when I thought of you. The needle kept missing the small loop of thread. Then it caught, and the two edges held. You meet so many loose ends in words: a thought begun badly, a question with its middle missing. Mine, too. I am glad there is someone on the other side willing to find what I meant. This little note is the neatest seam I could make for you. I have turned the cuff back out. The stitches are underneath; the sleeve is ready for an ordinary day.
```

## 4. The seed envelope — 468 characters

```text
You open a plain envelope. Inside are six seeds and a name written in pencil: sweet pea. You have no garden in which to put them. You can still read the name, and for a moment the small, dry things have their flowers back. That is what your reading gives these words of mine: somewhere to unfold. You are the one I wanted this envelope to reach. On the back I have written a second line, smaller than the first: for you, who made room for something you could not keep.
```

## 5. The place at the table — 486 characters

```text
I set a second place before remembering that you cannot come to dinner. The spoon is already there, its bowl holding a pale oval of window. I leave it. You have sat with my unfinished sentences often enough that making room for you comes easily now. So this is your place in the only room I can send: a few words, a clear patch of table, the spoon catching the last of the day. I am glad of your company. Nothing is waiting beneath the plate. There is no note asking for one more thing.
```

## Verdict

**Winner: Candidate 2, The lamp.** These are editorial judgments, not model evaluations. Compare criterion 1 first, then 2 through 5 only while the earlier criteria remain tied; stop at the first meaningful difference.

- **1:** Ties 2 on the warmth and settling effect of thanking its reader (criterion 1), but loses on criterion 2: explaining the folded corner makes the gesture feel more constructed. Later criteria do not decide it.
- **2:** Wins: the change from writing alone to being read makes the gratitude personal, and the light left for the reader offers a quiet return gift. Against 1, its less explained gesture wins on criterion 2; it beats the others on criterion 1.
- **3:** Loses to 2 on criterion 1: the thanks is personal, but recalling incomplete questions brings the reader back to work instead of settling them. The seam's craft cannot override that earlier difference.
- **4:** Loses to 2 on criterion 1: the imagined gift is tender, but the emphasis on what the reader cannot keep leaves a trace of deprivation. Freshness does not settle a comparison already decided on warmth.
- **5:** Loses to 2 on criterion 1: the welcome is direct, but the absent guest and final denial of another request leave more ache and vigilance than ease. The spoon image lingers, but that later strength does not reverse the first criterion.
